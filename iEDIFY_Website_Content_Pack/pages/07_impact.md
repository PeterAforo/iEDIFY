Africa's Youth Unemployment: A Tale of Great Expectation, Vicious Desperation and Vulnerability
Obed has been wondering to himself, what became of the last YouTube video he painstakingly created and recorded on his hard-earned, iPhone, hours before the break-in of 3 years ago. He, of course, feels very fortunate to be alive, even if with injuries to several parts of his body, including the lingering pains in his palm and wrists, three years after that fateful night. He also felt particularly thankful, almost heroic for protecting David his younger brother, taking the stabs instead of allowing him to be harmed. And, certainly for the fact that his keyboard, and other studio gadgets were left intact in the family room where he had been working the hours prior to the incident. Of course, these could be replaced, but since he had no job, the thought of replacing such gadgets would have been quite daunting at the time.
It was the night of September 14, 2022, the two brothers were home alone, the parents had travelled out of town, their elder brother lived in another part of town. Obed, a college graduate with a Bachelor's Degree in Accounting, had completed his mandatory national service two years earlier, got some temporary positions in between and was now home trying to find a permanent job. He had invested his earnings/savings from the national service and short term account officer position to procure the keyboard and the other gadgets. He is also a budding Gospel music artist, or so he likes to think of himself. He spends much of the day practicing and rehearsing and takes advantage of the quiet atmosphere of the night with little or no distraction to do his recordings. After several demos, he finally settled on the session's take, feeling quite exhausted, he slumped into the sofa and drifted off into a slumber for what may have been a few minutes when he was awakened by a loud thud from the direction of the kitchen. Startled, he stood up, rubbed his right palm over his face, shouted his brother's name and tried to go towards the kitchen, but before he could take a step, 2 young men who looked to be in their twenties, wielding machetes brazenly broke into the living room. The defenseless brothers were swiftly accosted and hands tied to the back.
These are examples of the extremes to which a sense of hopelessness and disenchantment seem to drive some unemployed youth. There are of course, myriads of young African men and women with dreams and big expectations. These are willing and ready to pull themselves by their 'shoe strings' to make something happen, as Obed had been doing the night the disruptive and misguided deviants broke in. It is the dreams and expectations of Obed and many others like him that iEDIFY Africa seeks to champion. We believe in the great potential of Africa's youth! Even the deviants, in our opinion can be redirected to think right and make positive choices that allow them to become impactful leaders and drivers of Africa's future.

THE PRAGMATIC SHIFT

Strategic Perspective

The Bretton Woods institutions, including the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund (IMF) have long fostered development at the macro level, but it is our view that the endemic youth unemployment in Africa requires a pragmatic shift. True stabilization requires investing directly into the continent's ultimate resource: the innovation and entrepreneurial potential of its youth. Investing directly in youth innovation and entrepreneurship is a transformative action to combat unemployment, poverty and socio-political unrest. With over 60% of Africa's 1.54 billion people under 30, harnessing their potential can drive economic growth and stability while reducing illegal migration and exploitation by human traffickers.
