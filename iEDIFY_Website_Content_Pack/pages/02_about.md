OUR REFLECTIONS

Africa's Youth Unemployment: A Tale of Great Expectation, Vicious Desperation and Vulnerability
Obed has been wondering to himself, what became of the last YouTube video he painstakingly created and recorded on his hard-earned, iPhone, hours before the break-in of 3 years ago. He, of course, feels very fortunate to be alive, even if with injuries to several parts of his body, including the lingering pains in his palm and wrists, three years after that fateful night. He also felt particularly thankful, almost heroic for protecting David his younger brother, taking the stabs instead of allowing him to be harmed. And, certainly for the fact that his keyboard, and other studio gadgets were left intact in the family room where he had been working the hours prior to the incident. Of course, these could be replaced, but since he had no job, the thought of replacing such gadgets would have been quite daunting at the time.
It was the night of September 14, 2022, the two brothers were home alone, the parents had travelled out of town, their elder brother lived in another part of town. Obed, a college graduate with a Bachelor's Degree in Accounting, had completed his mandatory national service two years earlier, got some temporary positions in between and was now home trying to find a permanent job. He had invested his earnings/savings from the national service and short term account officer position to procure the keyboard and the other gadgets. He is also a budding Gospel music artist, or so he likes to think of himself. He spends much of the day practicing and rehearsing and takes advantage of the quiet atmosphere of the night with little or no distraction to do his recordings. After several demos, he finally settled on the session's take, feeling quite exhausted, he slumped into the sofa and drifted off into a slumber for what may have been a few minutes when he was awakened by a loud thud from the direction of the kitchen. Startled, he stood up, rubbed his right palm over his face, shouted his brother's name and tried to go towards the kitchen, but before he could take a step, 2 young men who looked to be in their twenties, wielding machetes brazenly broke into the living room. The defenseless brothers were swiftly accosted and hands tied to the back.
They started menacingly rummaging and scattering everything, demanding money, phones, laptops, any portable item of value they can find, while all the time brandishing the machetes. Once they located the iPhone used for the recording, they demanded that Obed 'unlocks' it before they took it from him. With quick thinking, as he 'fumbled' to 'unlock', he sent his senior brother a quick alert! For the next several minutes, the assailants, who were unmasked and clearly under the influence of drugs started to assault them with the machetes. Obed on several occasions threw himself between the knife brandishing leader of the duo and his younger brother David, serving as a human shield and taking the stabs and slashes in the process. When the knife was held to his cheek, he almost 'resigned' himself to the very real possibility of being killed over such items as phones, money, laptops and wristwatches. While held at knife point by the older of the two thugs, the other one emerged from one of the rooms with a bag into which he gathered their loot of the night. As for the iPhone, once the 'unlocking' was completed, the lead assailant took and pocketed it rather than add it to the other items in the bag!
As soon as he received his brother's 'mayday' message, the senior brother quickly placed calls to the elders of the Church where he was the Resident Pastor and within moments a response team of an Elder and a couple of young men arrived. Realizing they were no longer alone in the house with their victims, the burglars hurriedly took off, the one with the bag full of stolen items seemed to panic and dropped the bag as he dashed off. They did succeed in making off with the iPhone and the recording. Once in, the rescue team untied the young men and took them to the hospital where Obed received several stitches for the gashes in his right palm, forearm, shoulder and two fingers.
When they got to the local Police Station some hours later, they met another family of young men whose home had been broken into and from their descriptions of the appearance and behavior of the assailants, it seemed certain that it was the same duo who had been on their armed robbery operation that night in two neighborhoods of the same municipality.
These are examples of the extremes to which a sense of hopelessness and disenchantment seem to drive some unemployed youth. There are of course, myriads of young African men and women with dreams and big expectations. These are willing and ready to pull themselves by their 'shoe strings' to make something happen, as Obed had been doing the night the disruptive and misguided deviants broke in. It is the dreams and expectations of Obed and many others like him that iEDIFY Africa seeks to champion. We believe in the great potential of Africa's youth! Even the deviants, in our opinion can be redirected to think right and make positive choices that allow them to become impactful leaders and drivers of Africa's future.

OUR CONCEPT OF INTERVENTION

The International Entrepreneurial Development and Innovation Fund for Youth in Africa (iEDIFY Africa) Initiative: The iEDIFY Africa is an innovative concept aimed at contributing to ongoing efforts at finding long-term and sustainable options for addressing the continent's youth unemployment. We will deploy tools of advocacy, training/capacity building and resource/fund mobilization to enhance the entrepreneurial and employment prospects of young people as they transition from school into the world of work. The program will introduce young people to new ways of thinking about national development and their potential role and contribution to economic development and prosperous future for themselves. The agency will work to identify and collaborate with key stakeholders and actors across the spectrum to provide education/training and capacity building and facilitate strategic funding mechanisms to enable the generation of seed for program participants. Advocacy efforts will target policy decision-makers and the broad range of stakeholders and actors with a view to raising awareness and advocating for innovative policy and action that help to address long term issues of funding gaps related to youth unemployment/underemployment and the attendant challenges.

OUR VISION

Our vision is that of "a thriving Africa where empowered and innovative youth drive sustainable development and transform communities through innovation and entrepreneurship."

OUR MISSION

To educate, train and equip African youth with essential skills, resources and funding to create sustainable businesses, overcome unemployment and poverty and lead impactful change through entrepreneurship.

Strategic Perspective

The Bretton Woods institutions, including the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund (IMF) have long fostered development at the macro level, but it is our view that the endemic youth unemployment in Africa requires a pragmatic shift. True stabilization requires investing directly into the continent's ultimate resource: the innovation and entrepreneurial potential of its youth. Investing directly in youth innovation and entrepreneurship is a transformative action to combat unemployment, poverty and socio-political unrest. With over 60% of Africa's 1.54 billion people under 30, harnessing their potential can drive economic growth and stability while reducing illegal migration and exploitation by human traffickers.

PROGRAM PILLARS

Four Pillars of Change
01
Advocacy, Awareness Raising and Sensitization

We engage with key stakeholders across the spectrum – including government agencies, policy/decision makers, political actors, diplomatic communities (particularly heads of African missions and other influential personalities in the international development arena), entrepreneurs/industry leaders, youth groups, civil society, opinion leaders, parents/guardian communities, educators and the media among others – to facilitate public discourse on the establishment of a revolving Trust Fund to support youth innovation and entrepreneurship in Africa.

02
Establishment of a Sustainable Youth Innovation & Entrepreneurship Trust Fund

We aim to mobilize funds from international donors, private investors, and development partners to enable the creation of a revolving Fund that helps to provide vital seed capital for youth-led startups.

03
Integrate Innovation and Entrepreneurship into Education

We will partner with key educational stakeholders to mainstream entrepreneurship and innovation into secondary and postsecondary school curricula across the continent.

04
Launch a Seed Funding Scheme for Youth Startups

We will design and implement an accessible platform to provide young African innovators and entrepreneurs with the funding and support needed to turn their business concepts into reality.
