Captured from the live public website on 2 October 2026 for redevelopment.

## Coverage

- 14 public pages, including sign-in and registration copy.
- 15 expanded team biographies, beyond the short team cards.
- 24 distinct image files from 26 captured image references, including logos, illustrations and portraits.
- Source page URLs, link labels and destinations, image references and alt text.
- Support widget greeting and suggested questions.

This is a public content capture, not a source-code or database backup. It preserves published wording, including inconsistencies. Navigation and footers repeat in page captures to avoid silently removing content. Form text includes dropdown options and labels exposed in the rendered page; the apparent Company fax and Department code anti-spam fields need developer verification before being reproduced as user-facing fields.

## Limits

Private community posts, messages, member data, admin/CMS content, unpublished drafts, application records and email mailboxes were not accessed. No sign-in or forms were submitted. Original unoptimized media, source code, database exports, full downloadable document collections and unlinked pages are not guaranteed by this capture. The image files are the variants served to this browser. No additional document download links were found in the captured Publications links; obtain original documents from the client. The public pages do not establish that proposed new program and funding modules already exist.

OUR REFLECTIONS

Africa's Youth Unemployment: A Tale of Great Expectation, Vicious Desperation and Vulnerability
Obed has been wondering to himself, what became of the last YouTube video he painstakingly created and recorded on his hard-earned, iPhone, hours before the break-in of 3 years ago. He, of course, feels very fortunate to be alive, even if with injuries to several parts of his body, including the lingering pains in his palm and wrists, three years after that fateful night. He also felt particularly thankful, almost heroic for protecting David his younger brother, taking the stabs instead of allowing him to be harmed. And, certainly for the fact that his keyboard, and other studio gadgets were left intact in the family room where he had been working the hours prior to the incident. Of course, these could be replaced, but since he had no job, the thought of replacing such gadgets would have been quite daunting at the time.
It was the night of September 14, 2022, the two brothers were home alone, the parents had travelled out of town, their elder brother lived in another part of town. Obed, a college graduate with a Bachelor's Degree in Accounting, had completed his mandatory national service two years earlier, got some temporary positions in between and was now home trying to find a permanent job. He had invested his earnings/savings from the national service and short term account officer position to procure the keyboard and the other gadgets. He is also a budding Gospel music artist, or so he likes to think of himself. He spends much of the day practicing and rehearsing and takes advantage of the quiet atmosphere of the night with little or no distraction to do his recordings. After several demos, he finally settled on the session's take, feeling quite exhausted, he slumped into the sofa and drifted off into a slumber for what may have been a few minutes when he was awakened by a loud thud from the direction of the kitchen. Startled, he stood up, rubbed his right palm over his face, shouted his brother's name and tried to go towards the kitchen, but before he could take a step, 2 young men who looked to be in their twenties, wielding machetes brazenly broke into the living room. The defenseless brothers were swiftly accosted and hands tied to the back.
They started menacingly rummaging and scattering everything, demanding money, phones, laptops, any portable item of value they can find, while all the time brandishing the machetes. Once they located the iPhone used for the recording, they demanded that Obed 'unlocks' it before they took it from him. With quick thinking, as he 'fumbled' to 'unlock', he sent his senior brother a quick alert! For the next several minutes, the assailants, who were unmasked and clearly under the influence of drugs started to assault them with the machetes. Obed on several occasions threw himself between the knife brandishing leader of the duo and his younger brother David, serving as a human shield and taking the stabs and slashes in the process. When the knife was held to his cheek, he almost 'resigned' himself to the very real possibility of being killed over such items as phones, money, laptops and wristwatches. While held at knife point by the older of the two thugs, the other one emerged from one of the rooms with a bag into which he gathered their loot of the night. As for the iPhone, once the 'unlocking' was completed, the lead assailant took and pocketed it rather than add it to the other items in the bag!
As soon as he received his brother's 'mayday' message, the senior brother quickly placed calls to the elders of the Church where he was the Resident Pastor and within moments a response team of an Elder and a couple of young men arrived. Realizing they were no longer alone in the house with their victims, the burglars hurriedly took off, the one with the bag full of stolen items seemed to panic and dropped the bag as he dashed off. They did succeed in making off with the iPhone and the recording. Once in, the rescue team untied the young men and took them to the hospital where Obed received several stitches for the gashes in his right palm, forearm, shoulder and two fingers.
When they got to the local Police Station some hours later, they met another family of young men whose home had been broken into and from their descriptions of the appearance and behavior of the assailants, it seemed certain that it was the same duo who had been on their armed robbery operation that night in two neighborhoods of the same municipality.
These are examples of the extremes to which a sense of hopelessness and disenchantment seem to drive some unemployed youth. There are of course, myriads of young African men and women with dreams and big expectations. These are willing and ready to pull themselves by their 'shoe strings' to make something happen, as Obed had been doing the night the disruptive and misguided deviants broke in. It is the dreams and expectations of Obed and many others like him that iEDIFY Africa seeks to champion. We believe in the great potential of Africa's youth! Even the deviants, in our opinion can be redirected to think right and make positive choices that allow them to become impactful leaders and drivers of Africa's future.

OUR CONCEPT OF INTERVENTION

The International Entrepreneurial Development and Innovation Fund for Youth in Africa (iEDIFY Africa) Initiative: The iEDIFY Africa is an innovative concept aimed at contributing to ongoing efforts at finding long-term and sustainable options for addressing the continent's youth unemployment. We will deploy tools of advocacy, training/capacity building and resource/fund mobilization to enhance the entrepreneurial and employment prospects of young people as they transition from school into the world of work. The program will introduce young people to new ways of thinking about national development and their potential role and contribution to economic development and prosperous future for themselves. The agency will work to identify and collaborate with key stakeholders and actors across the spectrum to provide education/training and capacity building and facilitate strategic funding mechanisms to enable the generation of seed for program participants. Advocacy efforts will target policy decision-makers and the broad range of stakeholders and actors with a view to raising awareness and advocating for innovative policy and action that help to address long term issues of funding gaps related to youth unemployment/underemployment and the attendant challenges.

OUR VISION

Our vision is that of "a thriving Africa where empowered and innovative youth drive sustainable development and transform communities through innovation and entrepreneurship."

OUR MISSION

To educate, train and equip African youth with essential skills, resources and funding to create sustainable businesses, overcome unemployment and poverty and lead impactful change through entrepreneurship.

Strategic Perspective

The Bretton Woods institutions, including the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund (IMF) have long fostered development at the macro level, but it is our view that the endemic youth unemployment in Africa requires a pragmatic shift. True stabilization requires investing directly into the continent's ultimate resource: the innovation and entrepreneurial potential of its youth. Investing directly in youth innovation and entrepreneurship is a transformative action to combat unemployment, poverty and socio-political unrest. With over 60% of Africa's 1.54 billion people under 30, harnessing their potential can drive economic growth and stability while reducing illegal migration and exploitation by human traffickers.

Our Operational Framework

Facilitate an integrated and a comprehensive synergistic ecosystem where policy enables capital, education builds capacity and regional hubs deliver localized impact at scale. Implementation to center around four core program pillars, namely:

Policy & Trust Fund Advocacy

AT A GLANCE

iEDIFY Africa
Theory of Change

A Strategic Imperative — Addressing youth unemployment through a comprehensive, multi-pronged approach.

The Challenge

Africa's 1.5+ billion population is the youngest globally, with over 60% under age 30. By 2030, 375 million youth risk underemployment or unemployment.

The Cost of Inaction
Economic stagnation, mounting debt burden & dependency
Irregular migration & human trafficking
Regional instability
Lost GDP growth potential among others
The Opportunity

AfCFTA presents the largest emerging trade bloc globally — over fifty countries, creating a generational opportunity for youth-led enterprise across borders.

54 Countries
1.5B People
$3.4T GDP
The Solution

A $100 million Youth Innovation & Entrepreneurship Trust Fund — a sustainable, revolving financing and capacity-building mechanism

HOW WE WORK

Operational Framework

Facilitate an integrated and a comprehensive synergistic ecosystem where policy enables capital, education builds capacity and regional hubs deliver localized impact at scale. Implementation to center around four core program pillars, namely:

Innovation

Fostering creative solutions for youth entrepreneurship and sustainable development.

Sustainability

Building long-term, self-sustaining programs that outlast initial funding cycles.

Education

Integrating innovation and entrepreneurship into formal and informal education systems.

Inclusion

Ensuring youth participation across gender, geography, and socioeconomic status.

Policy & Trust Fund Advocacy

Drive institutional sustainability through strategic policy engagement and advocacy.

Capitalization & Fund Management

Establish independent robust governance and financial architecture that ensure transparency, accountability, and sustainable capital deployment.

Education & Curriculum Integration

Work in partnership with educational stakeholders and industry leaders across the spectrum to build entrepreneurial capacity through training and practical skills development tailored to African market realities.

Seed Funding & Regional Hubs

Provide localized support through regional innovation hubs offering mentorship, networking, market access, and direct seed capital to high-potential ventures.

REAL STORIES, REAL STAKES

Africa's Youth Unemployment: A Tale of Great Expectation, Vicious Desperation and Vulnerability
Obed has been wondering to himself, what became of the last YouTube video he painstakingly created and recorded on his hard-earned, iPhone, hours before the break-in of 3 years ago. He, of course, feels very fortunate to be alive, even if with injuries to several parts of his body, including the lingering pains in his palm and wrists, three years after that fateful night. He also felt particularly thankful, almost heroic for protecting David his younger brother, taking the stabs instead of allowing him to be harmed. And, certainly for the fact that his keyboard, and other studio gadgets were left intact in the family room where he had been working the hours prior to the incident. Of course, these could be replaced, but since he had no job, the thought of replacing such gadgets would have been quite daunting at the time.
It was the night of September 14, 2022, the two brothers were home alone, the parents had travelled out of town, their elder brother lived in another part of town. Obed, a college graduate with a Bachelor's Degree in Accounting, had completed his mandatory national service two years earlier, got some temporary positions in between and was now home trying to find a permanent job. He had invested his earnings/savings from the national service and short term account officer position to procure the keyboard and the other gadgets. He is also a budding Gospel music artist, or so he likes to think of himself. He spends much of the day practicing and rehearsing and takes advantage of the quiet atmosphere of the night with little or no distraction to do his recordings. After several demos, he finally settled on the session's take, feeling quite exhausted, he slumped into the sofa and drifted off into a slumber for what may have been a few minutes when he was awakened by a loud thud from the direction of the kitchen. Startled, he stood up, rubbed his right palm over his face, shouted his brother's name and tried to go towards the kitchen, but before he could take a step, 2 young men who looked to be in their twenties, wielding machetes brazenly broke into the living room. The defenseless brothers were swiftly accosted and hands tied to the back.
These are examples of the extremes to which a sense of hopelessness and disenchantment seem to drive some unemployed youth. There are of course, myriads of young African men and women with dreams and big expectations. These are willing and ready to pull themselves by their 'shoe strings' to make something happen, as Obed had been doing the night the disruptive and misguided deviants broke in. It is the dreams and expectations of Obed and many others like him that iEDIFY Africa seeks to champion. We believe in the great potential of Africa's youth! Even the deviants, in our opinion can be redirected to think right and make positive choices that allow them to become impactful leaders and drivers of Africa's future.

THE PRAGMATIC SHIFT

Strategic Perspective

The Bretton Woods institutions, including the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund (IMF) have long fostered development at the macro level, but it is our view that the endemic youth unemployment in Africa requires a pragmatic shift. True stabilization requires investing directly into the continent's ultimate resource: the innovation and entrepreneurial potential of its youth. Investing directly in youth innovation and entrepreneurship is a transformative action to combat unemployment, poverty and socio-political unrest. With over 60% of Africa's 1.54 billion people under 30, harnessing their potential can drive economic growth and stability while reducing illegal migration and exploitation by human traffickers.

## Margaret Alexander Rehoboth

INITIATOR AND LEAD STRATEGIST

Margaret Alexander Rehoboth

Margaret is an accomplished development practitioner and a visionary with over two decades of experience in furthering accountability to the interests of women/girls and other marginalized constituencies in development policy and action. She has worked to facilitate economic opportunities for women and contributed to increasing their representation, participation and actual involvement in leadership, governance and peace processes in society.

As a Course Director at the Kofi Annan International Peacekeeping Training Center, Margaret was responsible for the postconflict reconstruction training courses in Election Observation and Election Management. She also served as the Head of the Women, Peace and Security Institute (WPSI) where she had responsibility for the day-to-day running of the Institute, providing strategic leadership, technical expertise and managerial oversight of the WPSI program. She provided technical expertise and guidance for the institutional mainstreaming of gender at the Centre and led the Institute to collaborate with the Office of the AU Chair's Special Envoy on Women, Peace and Security to train high ranking African Women in Election Observation and Dispute Resolution. Under her leadership, the WPSI initiated and successfully conducted the Centre's first accredited domestic election observation mission during the 2016 general elections in Ghana. She has served as a facilitator for Executive Training Courses in Politics, Peace and Security as Knutsford University where she was responsible for the module on Gender, Peace & Development in the African Context.

She was part of the technical team that supported the November 2015 South Sudan Women's National Peace Dialogue to elaborate a position document and plan for monitoring the implementation of the agreement to end the conflict in that country. More recently, she worked to promote gender responsiveness in education, focusing on responsiveness to the learning needs of male and female basic school learners.

Prior to joining the KAIPTC, she variously served as an Adjunct Lecturer, teaching courses in Communication & Public Speaking and Development Studies, Research/Teaching Assistant, Team Leader and Program Coordinator among others. She has participated in a number of international development conferences and training workshops in various capacities – resource person/facilitator, rapporteur and participant.

Margaret has a multidisciplinary educational background that includes graduate work in GENDER and DEVELOPMENT and COMMUNICATION STUDIES from IDS, Sussex University and University of Ghana among others. She brings to iEDIFY Africa a depth of insight, passion and a desire to contribute to efforts at stemming Africa's mounting youth unemployment the attendant challenges.

## Mrs. Florence Freda Dennis

ADVISORY BOARD MEMBER

Mrs. Florence Freda Dennis

Mrs. Florence Freda Dennis was the first Division Manager – Integrity and Prevention, in the Office of Integrity and Anti-Corruption of the African Development Bank (AfDB) with over 30 years' experience in the Maritime Industry, Governance and Anti-Corruption work on the continent and beyond.

At the Bank she led a team that developed preventive measures to proactively reduce the potential for misconduct, fraud and corruption in AfDB financed operations focusing on due diligence, integrity compliance monitoring, training and capacity building for staff and key stakeholders. She has worked with a team to develop policy documents such as the Africa Integrity Fund, the Integrity Due Diligence Policy, Illicit Financial Flow Policy, Bank Group Due Diligence Guidelines and Screening Procedures on Anti-Money Laundering and Countering Financing of Terrorism and Counter Illicit Financial Flows, Guidelines on Compliance Monitoring for Debarred Entities and Project Integrity Reviews amongst others. At the Bank she contributed to the development of several projects in the Non-Sovereign Operations of the Bank and also contributed to enhance several country projects on Sovereign Operations. Her work over the years in the Bank has helped to streamline Bank operations in the Regional Member Countries through building capacity of Project Implementation Units and Government Officials on MDB Projects. She also assisted several MNCs, SMEs from USA, Canada, Asia and the African Continent to strengthen their internal compliance processes to meet their debarment conditions.

She further presented several policy papers at international conferences including the International Bar Association Annual Conference, C5 Anticorruption Conference, Annual Conference of International Investigators, OECD Integrity Forum, Financial Action Task Force (FATF), Anticorruption Hunters' Alliance conference amongst others. She retired in March 2024 after 10 years of fruitful service to the Bank, the continent and beyond Africa.

Prior to joining the African Development Bank in 2014, she managed Ghana Anti-Corruption Coalition for 10 years bringing together the private sector, civil society and public governance institutions at the national, regional and continental level in the fight against corruption. She worked on research, advocacy and capacity building on anticorruption and governance. She was a technical expert for the UNCAC review process in Ghana, formed the Business Integrity Forum bringing together Private Multi-national Businesses and Public Institutions for collective action against corruption and the promotion of effective compliance processes in businesses.

Her work on Collective Action gained recognition by the World Bank Institute leading to the implementation of contract monitoring projects in Sierra Leone, Nigeria, Ghana and Liberia, building national efforts for accountability and effective public service delivery.

At GACC she contributed to the development and passage of the Whistleblower Act of Ghana and an active participant in the development of National Anti-Corruption Action Plan of Ghana (NACAP - 1) and ensured that Anti-Corruption and Governance were mainstreamed into the National Development Plan.

She also worked with development partners such as the OECD, GIZ, and UNODC, UNDP and the World Bank on anticorruption policy research, training and awareness and collective action among SMEs.

She began her career in the maritime industry in 1994, managing Ship Owners and Agents Association of Ghana and later joined P & O Nedlloyd West Africa as Commercial Manager.

Since retirement, she has been providing Consultancy Services on ad hoc basis to the Complaints, Evaluation and Integrity Unit (CEIU) of the Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) to develop Integrity Preventive measures in their operations.

She holds a first degree in Social Sciences (Economics & Geography) from the University of Science and Technology, Ghana, a Masters in Shipping Management from the World Maritime University, Malmö, Sweden, a Masters in Anti-Corruption Studies from the International Anti-Corruption Academy in Vienna, Austria and several diplomas in governance and anticorruption.

She is also a Professional Life Coach from the Institute of Life Coach Training. Her hobbies are cooking, interior decoration and swimming.

## Colonel Leonhard Hirschmann, German Army (Rtd.)

ADVISORY BOARD MEMBER

Colonel Leonhard Hirschmann, German Army (Rtd.)

Colonel Hirschmann retired in August 2020 from his assignment as Special Advisor to the Commander of NATO Joint Force Training Centre, Bydgoszcz, Poland after having served the German Armed Forces for more than 46 years.

Colonel Leo Hirschmann joined the German Armed Forces in 1974 as a paratrooper. He graduated from several courses at the German Armed Forces Command and Staff College, the Netherlands Armed Forces Staff College, the United States Infantry School, the United States Defense University and the NATO School.

He served in his home country in different command and staff positions during the Cold War Era and was in command of a Parachute Infantry Battalion after the re-unification of East and West Germany. In the Federal Ministry of Defense, he worked in the areas of Small Arms and Light Weapons Proliferation Control, Disarmament, Demobilization & Reintegration, and Implementation and Development of Conventional Arms Control in Europe.

Colonel Hirschmann's distinguished service as an Army Officer included international assignments at the NATO Headquarters for Land Forces in Central Europe, Heidelberg, Germany, the NATO Joint Headquarters North, Stavanger, Norway, the NATO Joint Force Training Centre, Bydgoszcz, Poland and the Kofi Annan International Peacekeeping Training Centre (KAIPTC), Accra, Ghana.

His peacekeeping deployments included tours as Operations Officer, German Operation Refugee Relief Aid, Iran (1991), Deputy Director Joint Operations, NATO Operation Joint Endeavour, Bosnia-Herzegovina (1996) and Chief J5 Plans, NATO Operation Consistent Effort, Kosovo (2001).

In 2004, Colonel Hirschmann became the first German Senior National Representative and Head of Training at the newly established NATO Joint Force Training Centre and made a significant contribution to its successful development.

Colonel Hirschmann served as Director of Training at the KAIPTC, where he was responsible for the development and delivery of integrated peace support operations training for military, police/gendarmerie and civilians preparing for missions in crisis areas in West-Africa.

His experiences with African authorities, but especially his daily interactions with the locals in Accra and other places beyond the borders of Ghana have helped to expand his view of human experience and development practice. The experience continues to shape his worldview and informs his firm belief in the importance of investing in efforts at promoting youth employment as a way of lifting people and communities out of poverty.
